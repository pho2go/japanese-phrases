How to read the pronunciations
  • The red respelling under each phrase is written for American English speakers. Read it out loud, one syllable at a time, at an even pace. Japanese has no heavy stress like English does.
  • Vowels are short and pure: ah as in "father," ee as in "see," oo as in "food," eh as in "bed," oh as in "go." ah-ee is two quick beats, not "eye."
  • Silent-ish endings: in desu and -masu the final "u" nearly disappears, so say dess and mahs. Same for "shi" before a "t" or "k," as in shteh.
  • Long vowels (written ō, ū in romaji) are just held about twice as long. Toh vs. tohh can change the word.
  • Double consonants mean a tiny pause: kippu is keep-poo with a beat before the "p."
  • The Japanese "r" sounds like the quick "tt" in American "butter" or "water." Don't roll it or use a hard English R.
  • Tricky spellings: tsoo is the "ts" in "cats"; foo (for ふ) is halfway between "f" and "h"; "g" is always hard, as in "go."
  • Tap Hear it to hear a phrase from your device's Japanese voice, or Show to display it in big text for staff. Don't worry about being perfect, since effort is appreciated.
