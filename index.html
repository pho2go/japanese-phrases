How to read the pronunciations
  • The red respelling on each card is written for American English speakers. Read it out loud at an even pace, giving every syllable about the same weight. Japanese doesn't punch one syllable the way English does.
  • Vowels are short and pure: ah as in "father," ee as in "see," oo as in "food," eh as in "bed," oh as in "go." Where you see sigh or guy, that's the Japanese "ai," close to English "eye."
  • Swallowed vowels: the final "u" in desu and -masu nearly disappears, so say dess and mahs. The "i" in "shi" often drops too, which is why you'll see mahsh-tah and shteh.
  • Long vowels (ō and ū in romaji, written ohh here when it matters) are held about twice as long. Length can change the meaning of a word.
  • Double consonants get a tiny pause: kippu (ticket) is keep-poo with a beat before the "p."
  • The Japanese "r" is a light tap, like the "tt" in American "butter" or "water." Don't roll it, and don't use a hard English R.
  • Other sounds: tsoo starts like the end of "cats"; foo (ふ) is a soft blow between "f" and "h"; "g" is always hard, as in "go."
